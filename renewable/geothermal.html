Geothermal energy for heating and power

Published on July 28, 2026

Geothermal energy is one of the most dependable low-carbon resources because it uses the Earth’s internal heat rather than weather conditions to generate power or provide thermal energy. It is especially valuable in regions with volcanic activity, tectonic movement, or accessible hot water reservoirs, where energy can be extracted from deep underground and used for electricity, heating, and cooling. In addition to electricity production, geothermal heat can be used for district heating, greenhouse agriculture, aquaculture, and industrial processes, which makes it more versatile than many people realize. Its biggest advantage is consistency: geothermal systems can provide power around the clock, unlike solar and wind which fluctuate with sunlight and wind availability. This makes geothermal a strong complement to variable renewable sources, helping utilities maintain reliability while reducing emissions. The technology is well established in some countries, but it still requires careful management because overuse of a geothermal reservoir can reduce pressure and lower output over time. Drilling and exploration can also be expensive, and not all locations have the geological conditions needed for commercial development. Even so, geothermal energy remains highly attractive for countries seeking energy independence and long-term stability. It also supports local jobs in engineering, drilling, plant operations, and maintenance. When combined with heat pumps and efficient building design, geothermal systems can reduce dependency on fossil fuels in both public infrastructure and private homes.
