Genetic Diversity and Seed Banks

Published on July 29, 2026

Genetic diversity is the raw material of evolution. It refers to the variety of inherited traits found within and between populations of species, and it is essential for adaptation, disease resistance, and long-term survival. When populations become small, isolated, or genetically uniform, they become less able to respond to environmental change. This is why conserving not only species but also their genetic diversity is so important for biodiversity protection.

Seed banks are one of the most practical tools for preserving genetic diversity, especially for plants. They store seeds under controlled conditions so that species can be protected even if wild populations decline. These collections help safeguard agricultural crops, medicinal plants, and threatened wild species that may be lost in their natural habitats. Seed banks are especially useful in the face of climate change and habitat destruction, because they preserve options for future restoration and research.

While seed banks are powerful, they cannot replace living ecosystems. They are most effective when paired with habitat protection and conservation in the wild. Maintaining genetic diversity therefore requires both in-situ conservation, such as preserving natural habitats, and ex-situ conservation, such as seed banks and botanical gardens. Together, these approaches provide a stronger safety net for biodiversity.
