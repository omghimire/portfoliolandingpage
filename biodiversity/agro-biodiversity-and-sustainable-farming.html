Agro-Biodiversity and Sustainable Farming

Published on July 29, 2026

Agro-biodiversity refers to the variety of plants, animals, microorganisms, and farming practices that support food production and rural livelihoods. It includes traditional crop varieties, livestock breeds, pollinators, soil organisms, and the knowledge farmers use to manage land over time. In a world dominated by industrial agriculture, agro-biodiversity is often overlooked, yet it is essential for resilient food systems. Diverse farming systems are more adaptable to pests, climate stress, and changing market conditions than monocultures.

Sustainable farming practices such as crop rotation, intercropping, agroforestry, organic soil management, and water-efficient irrigation can protect agro-biodiversity while maintaining productivity. These methods improve soil health, reduce chemical dependence, and create more habitats for beneficial insects and birds. They also help preserve local seed varieties and traditional farming knowledge that may otherwise disappear under pressure from modern commercial agriculture.

Protecting agro-biodiversity is important not only for food security but also for cultural identity and environmental health. Farmers who maintain diverse systems often contribute to pollination, pest control, and ecosystem resilience. Sustainable agriculture therefore supports biodiversity in fields, forests, and communities alike.
