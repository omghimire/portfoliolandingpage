The Greenhouse Effect Explained

Published on January 3, 2026

The greenhouse effect is a natural process that warms the Earth's surface. When the Sun's energy reaches the Earth's atmosphere, some of it is reflected back to space and the rest is absorbed and re-radiated by greenhouse gases. This absorbed energy warms the atmosphere and the Earth's surface. Without this natural effect, Earth's average temperature would be about -18°C instead of the current 15°C.

The problem arises when human activities increase the concentration of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere, enhancing the natural greenhouse effect. This enhanced effect traps more heat, leading to global warming and climate change. Understanding this mechanism is fundamental to addressing climate change and developing effective mitigation strategies.
