Paris Agreement & International Climate Policy

Published on January 3, 2026

The Paris Agreement, adopted in 2015 under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change, is a landmark international treaty that aims to limit global warming to well below 2°C, preferably to 1.5°C, compared to pre-industrial levels. It represents a global consensus on climate action and includes commitments from nearly all countries to reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

The Agreement operates on a five-year cycle of increasingly ambitious climate action. Countries submit Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) outlining their climate targets and strategies. The Agreement also includes provisions for financial support to help developing countries transition to low-carbon economies and adapt to climate impacts. It represents a fundamental shift in global climate governance.
