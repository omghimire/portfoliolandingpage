Sea Level Rise & Coastal Impacts

Published on January 3, 2026

Sea level rise is one of the most visible and consequential impacts of climate change. Global mean sea level has risen about 20 centimeters since 1900, with the rate of rise accelerating in recent decades. This rise is caused by thermal expansion of seawater as it warms and the addition of water from melting glaciers and ice sheets.

Coastal communities face increased risks from flooding, erosion, and saltwater intrusion into freshwater sources. Small island nations and low-lying coastal areas are particularly vulnerable, with some facing existential threats. Adaptation measures include building sea walls, elevating infrastructure, and in some cases, planned retreat from vulnerable areas. The economic and social costs of sea level rise are projected to be substantial.
