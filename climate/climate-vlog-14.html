Climate Feedback Loops (Albedo, Permafrost)

Published on January 3, 2026

Climate feedback loops are processes that can amplify or dampen the effects of climate change. Positive feedback loops accelerate warming, while negative feedback loops slow it down. Two critical positive feedback loops involve the albedo effect and permafrost thaw, both of which could significantly accelerate climate change if triggered.

The albedo feedback occurs when melting ice reduces the Earth's reflectivity, causing more solar energy to be absorbed and further warming. The permafrost feedback involves the release of methane and CO₂ from thawing permafrost, which contains vast amounts of stored carbon. These feedback loops are particularly concerning because they can become self-reinforcing and difficult to reverse once activated.
