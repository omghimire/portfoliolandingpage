Biodiversity Loss Linked to Climate Change

Published on January 3, 2026

Climate change is becoming a major driver of biodiversity loss, alongside habitat destruction, pollution, and overexploitation. Many species cannot adapt quickly enough to changing conditions, leading to range shifts, population declines, and in some cases, extinction. Coral reefs, polar ecosystems, and mountain species are particularly vulnerable to climate impacts.

The loss of biodiversity has cascading effects on ecosystem functioning and the services they provide, including pollination, water purification, and climate regulation. Protecting biodiversity requires both climate mitigation to reduce future impacts and conservation strategies that help species adapt. This includes creating climate corridors, protecting refugia, and reducing other stressors on ecosystems.
