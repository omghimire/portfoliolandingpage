Ocean Warming & Acidification

Published on January 3, 2026

The oceans have absorbed about 90% of the excess heat from global warming and about 30% of the carbon dioxide emitted by human activities. This has led to ocean warming and acidification, both of which have profound impacts on marine ecosystems. Ocean warming affects marine life distribution, coral reefs, and weather patterns.

Ocean acidification occurs as seawater absorbs CO₂, lowering its pH and making it more acidic. This process particularly affects organisms that build calcium carbonate shells or skeletons, such as corals, shellfish, and some plankton species. The combined effects of warming and acidification threaten the health of marine ecosystems and the services they provide, including fisheries and coastal protection.
